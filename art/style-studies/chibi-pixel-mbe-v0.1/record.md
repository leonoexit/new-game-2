## Initial reading

- The pixel reference is a collection of very small, stout, abstract sprites. The first generated board remained too detailed, especially the hedge and house; a second pass removed flowers, roof texture and fine architecture. The revised board is a clearer test of the requested abstraction, though whether it has enough personality remains open.
- The MBE reference combines rounded objects, deliberate breaks in colored outlines, tiny faces on suitable subjects, short baseline marks and sparse accents. The owner selected this direction for official development; each individual card image still needs its own identity/state and mobile-size check.
- Both studies keep the same card identity meanings: the Hedgerow is rooted, the Turnip Crop grows in soil, and the Farmhouse is a place. Neither assumes every card needs a mascot face.
- The preceding [editorial square-card study](../editorial-square-card-v0.1/record.md) was rejected as game art because it felt educational. Its angled top name label was briefly tried on the tableau, then replaced by a bottom name overlay in the still-open UI-67 trial. Complete square MBE images fill the square card box, and the original dark game UI remains in place.
- Japan-inspired art and setting remain future ideas, not project decisions.

## Generation prompts

### Chibi pixel, first attempt

> Use case: stylized-concept. Create ONE square style-study board for the farming card game Little Valley Cards. The attached pixel image is a STYLE REFERENCE ONLY: study its short chibi proportions, very large deliberate pixel clusters, simplified silhouettes, tiny facial features on appropriate living subjects, compact 3-tone materials, clear dark pixel outlines and cheerful pastel accents. Create three ORIGINAL, separate, equally sized game art motifs arranged in a clean 3-column row with ample white space and no panel borders: (1) Hedgerow: a low dense rooted berry bush with 3 blue berry clusters, clearly a bush rather than a held twig; (2) mature Turnip Crop: 3 stout white turnips with violet shoulders and leafy green tops emerging from one small soil patch; (3) Farmhouse: a tiny welcoming cream house with terracotta roof, blue shutters and front door. Keep each centered, compact, unmistakable at 160 pixels, and drawn with genuinely blocky native pixel construction, no smooth contours or anti-aliased painterly shading. Their charm comes from extreme abstraction and soft chibi proportions; never turn them into a textbook diagram or an educational poster. A tiny face may appear on a turnip if useful, but not on the building or hedgerow. No text, labels, numbers, logos, card frames, UI, scenery, horizon, stickers, proprietary characters, or recognizable objects copied from the reference. A simple white background. This is an exploratory visual comparison, not final game art.

### Chibi pixel, simplification pass

> Edit this existing three-motif Little Valley Cards PIXEL CHIBI style board. Preserve exact three subject identities and left-to-right order: rooted berry hedgerow, three mature turnips in soil, farmhouse. Make the pixel language MUCH MORE ABSTRACT AND CHIBI: pretend each motif was first drawn on a tiny 32x32 pixel grid and scaled up with perfect nearest-neighbor edges. Reduce every motif to 4-6 main masses and a maximum of 3 solid shades per material. Remove all tiny flowers, window panes, individual roof tiles, tiny soil dots, speckles, subtle gradients, and decorative shrubs. Make farmhouse squat, almost toy-like, with one door, one simple window, one solid roof shape. Hedgerow should be a low semicircular foliage blob with only THREE clearly separated blue berry circles. Three turnips should be short round bulbs with small chunky leaves. The left/right motifs should feel as intentionally cute and abstract as the center turnip, not detailed game asset illustrations. Keep the clean evenly spaced three-column board, simple white or transparent background, no labels or frame. No educational diagram, no infographic, no pixel filtering of smooth paintings. Native hard pixel clusters throughout.

### MBE-inspired rounded outline

> Use case: stylized-concept. Make ONE square style-study board for Little Valley Cards with three ORIGINAL isolated motifs in an equally spaced 3-column row, each on a clean white background with generous empty space. The attached image is a STYLE REFERENCE ONLY for the broad MBE-like visual principles: very simple rounded geometric silhouettes, a few bold colored contour strokes with intentional short gaps, bright flat palette, tiny friendly faces where appropriate, sparse little dashes/sparkles, compact baseline accents. Do not recreate its particular orange, ice cream, guava, potion, planetary objects, composition, lettering, or artist's exact work. Subjects in left-to-right order: (1) a LOW ROOTED HEDGEROW BUSH with three oversized blue berry bunches, unmistakably a shrub rather than loose berries or an upright twig; (2) THREE MATURE TURNIPS partly emerging from one small soil patch, round white roots with purple shoulders and oversized green leaves; (3) a tiny CREAM FARMHOUSE, terracotta roof, blue shutters and front door. At most one or two tiny facial marks on the turnips; hedgerow and farmhouse remain recognizable inanimate place/plant motifs without faces. Distill each to 4-6 big shapes, minimal details and playful rounded proportions. This must read as lively product/game icon art rather than an educational illustration or infographic. Consistent motif scale and visual weight. No labels, text, typography, logos, card frames, UI, charts, scenery, horizon, shadows, brand marks, or watermarks. Original exploratory work, not final game art.
