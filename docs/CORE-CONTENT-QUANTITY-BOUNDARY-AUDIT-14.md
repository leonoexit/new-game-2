## 1. Meaning of “minimum”

Minimum content means the smallest Year 1 paper package that represents every decision dimension currently claimed by the core baseline. It does **not** mean:

- every player must complete every Project or collect every Person;
- the final game may contain only these entries;
- fixture names become canonical;
- every template must appear as an active card;
- unused systems should receive filler content.

## 3. The remaining Project boundary

`PJ-X` is different from M/B:

- M reduces recurring Farm maintenance.
- B reduces a Farm burst peak.
- X opens an optional Upper-Grove context/source connection.

X is not required for Farm, Mine, Grove, River, collection or Person progress. Its source and recovery are safe, but paper evidence cannot decide whether demonstrating a third Project reward shape is worth the Year 1 content burden.

## 4. Two viable package arms

### `B2 — two-Project core`

Minimum Year 1 package contains M and B. X remains a deliberate paper comparator or later optional content, outside the minimum playable set.

What this communicates:

- Projects are primarily practical Farm improvements in the first content package.
- Mine supports materials, Depth, source discovery and optional native contexts without needing an infrastructure cross-link.
- Stone still has B/Sell/Keep allocation; Ore still has M/Sell/Keep allocation.

Benefits:

- lowest Project authoring and presentation load;
- no permit vocabulary needed in the minimum package;
- fewer simultaneous Project horizons;
- easier to lock M/B relief timing before committing to world-connection content.

Tradeoff:

- minimum content demonstrates only two capability-relief Projects;
- Project grammar may read as “Farm efficiency upgrades” rather than a broader world-change system;
- the tested Stone competition between B and X moves outside the minimum package.

### `B3 — three-shape Project core`

Minimum Year 1 package contains M, B and optional X.

What this communicates:

- Projects can improve maintenance, improve burst handling or open a new optional context;
- Project is a general world-change grammar, not only a Farm-upgrade grammar;
- Wood/Stone/Ore/Gold allocation crosses Producer and Connector interests.

Benefits:

- preserves all three tested Project reward shapes in the minimum package;
- gives Connector/Collector an infrastructure Project without making it a route gateway;
- makes the selected permit comparator and Stone refresh decision visible in actual Year 1 content.

Tradeoff:

- one more Project identity, requirement preview and reward context to author;
- permit fiction/vocabulary must be understandable even though its exact price remains a fixture;
- more simultaneous optional horizons and greater risk that players interpret three Projects as a checklist.
