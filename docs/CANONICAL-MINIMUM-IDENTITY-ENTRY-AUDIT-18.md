## 1. What L-I fixes and what it does not

`L-I` fixes the sequence: canonical identity/quantity work happens before exact numerical product lock. It does not make the current fixture vocabulary canonical.

The current names were created to keep paper logs readable:

- crops: Shortroot, Tallbean, Dewleaf;
- Catch: River Minnow, Rain Eel;
- non-crop/fish items: Hedgerow Berry, Fallen Wood, Plain Stone, Seam Ore, Wild Herb;
- Projects: Water Trough, Harvest Bench, Upper-Grove Footbridge;
- Persons: Mira, Nell, Bram;
- collection labels: Field Notes, `COL-1`, `COL-N`.

Their functional distinctions are supported; their names and fiction are not yet owner-approved canonical content.

## 3. Why quantity expansion is not the first identity action

The structural lower bound already represents every currently claimed core decision dimension. Adding another crop, Catch, source, Project or Person before the minimum identities are evaluated would make it unclear whether the entry serves a missing role or only increases volume.

Therefore the first L-I step should canonicalize or deliberately review the existing minimum slots. Intended Year 1 quantity beyond that lower bound becomes the next gate after the identity direction is known.

## 4. Two identity-entry arms

### `ID0 — promote current fixtures`

Treat the current paper names and assignments as canonical starting content:

- Shortroot/Tallbean/Dewleaf;
- River Minnow/Rain Eel;
- the five current source/item names;
- Water Trough/Harvest Bench/Upper-Grove Footbridge;
- Mira=A, Nell=C, Bram=K;
- Field Notes as the collection identity.

Benefits:

- fastest path to a canonical minimum set;
- preserves continuity with every paper trace and document;
- avoids a separate naming/comparison pass.

Tradeoff:

- promotes names invented for testing convenience without a deliberate world-identity comparison;
- locks the current Person-role assignments even though only A/C/K roles, not those assignments, were selected;
- may make later quantity expansion inherit placeholder-era naming patterns.

### `ID1 — deliberate canonical identity pass`

Keep every functional slot above fixed, but review candidate identities before promoting any current name or Person-role assignment.

The pass would:

1. define a small set of naming/world-identity criteria shared across families;
2. compare current fixtures against a limited number of alternatives;
3. change one identity variable at a time where practical;
4. ask the owner to approve canonical entries family by family;
5. leave all balance numbers as fixtures.

Benefits:

- separates mechanical evidence from world identity;
- lets the owner keep strong fixture names selectively rather than accepting or replacing the whole vocabulary;
- gives later content expansion a coherent naming and ownership pattern.

Tradeoff:

- requires additional paper-design and owner-review rounds;
- existing traces continue using fixture labels until each family is approved;
- does not itself answer the final Year 1 content count.

## 5. Owner-choice gate

| Choose | Meaning | Accepted tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ID0 — promote current fixtures` | Current names and Mira/Nell/Bram role assignments become the canonical minimum starting set | Faster, but identities created for testing receive no deliberate comparison |
| `ID1 — deliberate identity pass` | Preserve all functional slots while reviewing names, fiction and Person-role assignment before approval | More paper work and owner gates before quantity expansion |

`ID1` is recommended. L-I was selected specifically to avoid balancing around placeholders; deliberately checking whether those placeholders deserve promotion follows the same principle.
