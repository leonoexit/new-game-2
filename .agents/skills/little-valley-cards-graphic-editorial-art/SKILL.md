# Little Valley Cards Graphic Editorial Art

Use this skill to revisit the compact graphic language tested in [`editorial-square-card-v0.1`](../../../art/style-studies/editorial-square-card-v0.1/record.md). The owner found the first implementation attractive but rejected it for the game because it felt like educational content. Treat that result as a limitation to solve in an explicit study, never as permission to replace the active pixel art or the game's skin.

## Visual language

- Build a subject from large flat color blocks, a strong outer silhouette, thick dark contours, and one rhythmic repetition when it suits the subject.
- Use playful asymmetry and only a few meaningful details. Let the motif identify a bush, crop, place, item, or action before adding decorative marks.
- Keep the art distinct from the card frame. Title, Type, State, Action, badges and location strips belong to UI code, not the generated image.
- Avoid labels, diagrams, explanatory arrows, educational-poster composition, branded packaging, copied reference objects, and a mascot forced onto every subject.
- Keep Little Valley Cards identities intact. A Hedgerow is a rooted bush; a Crop remains a crop; a Farmhouse remains a place.

## Study workflow

Compare at least one plant, one crop, and one place at the same square display size. Review at roughly 160 × 160 logical pixels, then check whether the full illustration still has enough character. Record the source, complete prompt, delivered image, and `study`/`candidate` status under `art/style-studies/`.

Use this direction only for exploration until the owner explicitly approves it. Approval would also require a separate decision about typography and the wider game UI; a card-art study does not make that decision. The layout ideas in the linked study may be tested independently with other art styles.
